# Market Research

## Purpose

This file defines the research framework for the Free Marketing project.

The purpose is to build a reliable and repeatable system for researching markets, industries, applications, customer opportunities, competitors, and international markets for Alamir Pack.

This file defines **how research is conducted**. It does not contain the final research results.

---

## 1. What We Research

Research may cover:

* The Egyptian industrial market
* International markets and export opportunities
* New industrial sectors that may use paper cores or carton corner protectors
* New applications for existing products
* Potential customer types
* Customer needs and purchasing requirements
* Market demand and search demand
* Competitors and their market positioning
* Import and export activity
* Relevant industries and manufacturing activities
* Potential target countries
* Industry trends
* Distribution and sales channels
* Content and communication opportunities
* Commercial barriers and requirements

The central research question is:

> Where else can Alamir Pack's products solve a real industrial need?

---

## 2. Research Sources

Research should use reliable external sources such as:

* Government and official statistical sources
* Trade and customs data
* Industry associations
* Manufacturing directories
* B2B marketplaces
* Company websites
* Competitor websites
* Search engines
* Industry reports
* Professional publications
* LinkedIn and other professional networks
* Relevant customer discussions and communities

When possible, important findings should be confirmed using more than one reliable source.

---

## 3. Company Information Rule

All information about Alamir Pack itself must come from the official company source of truth:

**Start App / GitHub**

External research must not invent, modify, or override official company information.

External research is used to discover information outside the company, including:

* Markets
* Industries
* Competitors
* Customers
* Countries
* Demand
* Trends
* Applications
* Import/export activity
* Search behavior
* Commercial opportunities

---

## 4. Fact vs. Assumption

Every research finding should be treated as one of the following:

### Verified Fact

Supported by a reliable source or multiple sources.

### Research Finding

A conclusion derived from multiple pieces of evidence.

### Hypothesis

A possible opportunity that still needs validation.

### Unknown

Information that has not yet been confirmed.

We must never present a hypothesis as a verified fact.

---

## 5. New Opportunity Validation

A new industry, application, customer type, or country should not be considered a real opportunity simply because the product appears technically relevant.

Before considering an opportunity validated, research should look for evidence of:

* A real industrial need
* Identifiable potential customers
* Existing or potential product use
* Sufficient market activity
* Relevant product requirements
* Reasonable commercial feasibility
* Evidence of demand
* A realistic way to reach potential customers

The goal is to move from:

**Possible Opportunity → Research → Evidence → Validation → Test**

---

## 6. Research Output

Research should eventually produce practical outputs, not only information.

A useful research result should help answer:

* What is the opportunity?
* Why could it matter?
* Which product could be relevant?
* Who are the potential customers?
* Where are they located?
* What evidence supports the opportunity?
* What requirements may exist?
* Who are the competitors?
* What is still unknown?
* What should we test next?

---

## 7. Research Workflow

The standard workflow is:

**Question → Research → Sources → Evidence → Analysis → Hypothesis → Validation → Test → Results → Learning**

Research should always lead toward a practical next step whenever a realistic opportunity is identified.

---

## 8. Research Quality Rules

* Do not invent missing information.
* Clearly separate facts from assumptions.
* Record important sources.
* Prefer recent information when researching current markets.
* Use primary and authoritative sources whenever possible.
* Cross-check important findings.
* Record uncertainty when evidence is weak or incomplete.
* Do not assume that a large market automatically means a good opportunity.
* Do not assume that technical compatibility automatically means commercial demand.
* Do not treat a single source as conclusive when stronger verification is possible.

---

## 9. Relationship to the Marketing System

Research feeds the rest of the Free Marketing system.

**Research**
↓
**Identify Opportunities**
↓
**Define Target Customers**
↓
**Create Content and Offers**
↓
**Generate Leads**
↓
**Measure Results**
↓
**Learn and Improve**
↓
**Return to Research**

This creates a continuous learning system rather than a one-time marketing study.

## Important Principle

The goal is not to collect the largest amount of information.

The goal is to discover **real, evidence-based commercial opportunities** that can be tested and measured.
